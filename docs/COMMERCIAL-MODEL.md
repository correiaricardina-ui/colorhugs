# ColorHugs — Commercial Model

Three revenue lines, one content library, and the order they are built in.

Status: `[PROPOSAL]`. Prices are reasoned starting points, not researched
figures — they need validating against real buyers before anything is
published. Tax and licensing points need an accountant and a lawyer; they are
flagged, not answered.

---

## The insight this rests on

**It is one library, packaged three ways.**

A set of emotion cards, authored once, becomes:

| Packaged as | Sold to | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The content inside *How Do I Feel?* | families | subscription |
| A printable PDF pack | anyone | one-off purchase |
| A session-ready material with application notes | professionals | licence |

This is not three products competing for the same hours. It is one production
pipeline with three exits, and it changes what "finishing" a piece of content
means: **every piece is authored once, in a form that can leave through all
three doors.**

The consequence for everything we design from here: when we specify an activity,
we specify its printable and its professional note at the same time. Retrofitting
those later costs several times more than writing them alongside.

---

## Line A — Printables

**What it is.** Themed PDF packs a parent or professional buys once, downloads
and prints. Emotion card decks, a worry box to cut out and assemble, feelings
wheels, strengths cards, colouring sets, drawing mission cards.

**Why it goes first.** It needs no programming, no accounts, no moderation and
no AI. It can be earning while the platform is still being built. It also does
something the platform cannot yet do: **it tells you what people actually
want**, before you spend months turning a guess into an interactive activity.

**Proposed pricing**

| Product | Price | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single pack (10–20 pages) | €5–7 | the impulse purchase |
| Themed bundle (3–4 packs) | €15–20 | where most revenue comes from |
| A free pack | €0 | the sample that earns the email address |

**Margin.** Effectively 100% after authoring. No unit cost, no delivery cost,
no support cost.

**Where sold.** Two options, and I would do both. Your own site keeps the
margin and the customer relationship. A marketplace like Etsy brings buyers who
are already looking for exactly this, at the cost of a commission — treat it as
paid discovery, not as the main channel.

**The honest limit.** Printables are a crowded market and individual packs are
low-value. This line pays for tools and buys time; on its own it does not pay a
salary. Its real job is cash flow and market learning.

**First three packs to make** — drawn from where we are already working:

1. **Feelings deck** — the emotion families and their finer words, illustrated.
2. **My worry box** — a box to cut out and build, plus worry slips.
3. **Calm cards** — breathing and body-calming techniques, one per card.

## Line B — Professional licences

**This is the line I think is most underestimated, and probably the most
profitable per hour of your time.**

**Who buys.** Psychologists, speech and language therapists, occupational
therapists, special educational needs teachers, school psychologists, paediatric
services.

**Why they pay more, and stay.** A professional buys a tool for their work, not
a treat for their child. The purchase is a business expense, often
tax-deductible. They do not cancel because a six-year-old lost interest — they
cancel when they stop practising. **Churn is a fraction of consumer churn, and
willingness to pay is several times higher.**

**Scale, to show the shape of it.** Two hundred professionals at €15/month is
€36,000 a year. To reach the same with families at €5/month you need six hundred
active subscriptions — and families cancel far faster, so you must keep
replacing them.

**What they get, and this is the part that does not exist yet:**

- The full library, with permission to use it in paid professional practice
- **Application notes** on each material: what it is for, which ages, what to
  watch for, how to debrief. This is what separates a licence from a PDF, and
  **only you can write it.**
- Print-quality files with no ColorHugs promotional material on the child's copy
- The right to print and give copies to the families they work with

**Proposed pricing**

| Tier | Price | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual professional | €15/month or €150/year | one practitioner |
| Clinic (up to 5) | €45/month or €450/year | small practice |
| Institution | quote | school, hospital, municipality |

**Distribution is your network.** This is the part no competitor can copy. You
do not need advertising to reach Portuguese paediatric psychologists — you need
a mailing list, a talk at a conference, and a free sample pack that circulates
between colleagues.

**What it requires from the product.** A professional area, adult-facing, with
its own library, its own login and its own licence terms. **This is an eighth
area, and it is not in the current design.**

## Line C — Family subscription

Already designed: Free with the whole colouring library and the first three
items of most activities; Premium unlocking everything, sold monthly, yearly, or
weekly, with the per-month equivalent always shown.

**Proposed pricing**

| Plan | Price | Per month |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly | €6 | €6.00 |
| Yearly | €54 | €4.50 |
| Weekly | €2.50 | €10.80 |

The weekly option exists for a parent who wants a school holiday, not as the
default. Shown third, with its true monthly cost visible, as agreed.

**Why it is last.** It needs the engines built, the library filled and a name
people have heard of. It is the slowest to return and the most expensive to
grow, because acquiring family subscribers costs advertising money that does not
exist yet.

**When it becomes the biggest line**, it will be because the other two paid for
the years it took to get there.

## Sequence

**Phase 1 — Printables (now)**
Three packs and one free sample. A simple purchase page. Nothing else changes.
Goal: first revenue, and evidence of what sells.

**Phase 2 — Professional licences (once ~10 packs exist)**
Application notes written for each. Professional area built, licence terms
drafted, launch to your own network. Goal: recurring revenue that does not
depend on advertising.

**Phase 3 — Family subscription (once the engines and library exist)**
Accounts, payments, the Free/Premium split already designed. Goal: scale.

Each phase funds the next, and each reuses the content of the one before. **No
phase is thrown away when the next begins.**

---

## Costs that grow with success

Most of this business has near-zero marginal cost. Two features do not, and both
are the distinctive ones:

- **Imagine & Create** — every generated colouring page costs money. A heavy
  user could cost more than they pay. Premium-only and exempt from sampling,
  already decided; it will also need a fair-use ceiling, set on the adult
  account and invisible to the child.
- **Kids Draw submissions** — every drawing needs human review. That is your
  time, or someone's wage, per drawing. It does not scale for free.

**Before launching either, know what an active user costs.** These are the two
features that can turn growth into losses.

## Licensing — settle this before the first sale

Once material is out under vague terms, the terms cannot be tightened
retroactively. Proposed positions:

**Personal licence** (printables, family subscription)
Use with your own children, at home. Print as many copies as you need. Do not
redistribute, resell, or share the files.

**Professional licence**
Use with clients in your own practice. Print and give copies to the families you
work with. **Not** for sharing with colleagues, uploading to a clinic platform,
or reproducing in training materials. One licence per practitioner.

**Institutional licence**
Negotiated, named, and scoped to a stated number of practitioners or sites.

**Child-created artwork** is a separate question with its own consent problem,
already open as Q-016. Nothing a child makes is licensed to anyone until that is
answered with legal advice.

## What this changes in the product

1. **Printables become a first-class format**, not a nice extra at the end of an
   activity. Every activity we design from here specifies its printable.
2. **An eighth area exists**: the professional library. Adult-facing, separately
   licensed, with application notes.
3. **Every piece of content is authored three ways at once** — interactive
   content, printable, professional note. This is the single most important
   change to how we work from here.
4. **The production constraint is you, not engineering.** The engines are
   finite; the content is not. Anything that reduces your authoring time per
   piece is worth more than any feature.

## Open questions

### Q-018 `[OPEN QUESTION]` VAT and invoicing on digital sales
Selling digital products to consumers in the EU means charging VAT at the
customer's rate and filing through the OSS scheme, plus Portuguese invoicing
obligations. **This needs an accountant before the first sale**, not after. It
is not a large problem, but it is a real one and it is cheaper to set up
correctly than to correct.

### Q-019 `[OPEN QUESTION]` Prices
Every figure above is a reasoned starting point, not research. The professional
tier especially — ask five colleagues what they would pay for a licensed,
session-ready library with application notes. Their answer is worth more than my
estimate.

### Q-020 `[OPEN QUESTION]` Where printables are sold
Own site only, marketplace only, or both. Affects margin, discovery and how
soon the first sale can happen.

### Q-021 `[OPEN QUESTION]` Does the professional area share the ColorHugs brand?
A children's brand and a clinical tool sold to psychologists are different
registers. It may be right to keep one name, or to give the professional line
its own presentation under your own name — which already carries the credential
the professional buyer is buying.
