# ColorHugs — Certifications and Seals

A pre-launch goal: earn recognised marks that tell a parent this was checked by
someone other than us.

Status: `[PROPOSAL]`. Verified 2026-08-15 — **re-verify before applying**, as
programmes open, close and change eligibility.

---

## The good news first

**Almost everything these programmes ask for, ColorHugs has already decided to
do.** Verifiable parental consent rather than a tick box, no behavioural
advertising, no third-party trackers, data minimisation, no child-to-child
contact, human review before publication, a clear privacy policy.

That is not a coincidence — it is the same list. So pursuing certification is
mostly a matter of *documenting* what was built, rather than rebuilding it.
**That only stays true if it is designed in.** Retrofitting consent flows and
data practices to pass an audit is where these things become expensive.

## The three seals suggested — checked

### Selo de Segurança Digital / eSafety Label — **does not apply**

A European certification run in Portugal by the DGE. It certifies **schools** —
`agrupamentos de escolas` and standalone schools — on their digital safety
practices, policies and infrastructure, in gold, silver and bronze tiers. It is
also a requirement for the eTwinning School Label.

It is not awarded to websites, apps or companies. **ColorHugs cannot apply.**

*Where it could still matter:* if ColorHugs is ever used inside schools, being
a resource that helps a school meet its own eSafety Label criteria is a selling
point to that school. That is a positioning argument, not a certification.

Source: seguranet.pt/esafetylabel · esafetylabel.eu

### Selo Protetor (CNPDPCJ) — **applies to the practice, not to the website**

Run by the Comissão Nacional de Promoção dos Direitos e Proteção das Crianças e
Jovens. It distinguishes **ECMIJ** — entities with competence in child and
youth matters, across education, health, social action and security forces —
that implement good practice in promoting children's rights. It is built around
an eight-requirement integrated risk-management system, is valid for two years,
and is awarded to the *entity*, not to a product.

**So the candidate is Ricardina Correia's practice, not colorhugs.pt.** That is
still worth having: it is a Portuguese, state-backed mark of child-protection
practice, and it attaches to the name that endorses the platform — which is
exactly the credential the professional line is selling.

Applications run roughly March to June each year. The 9th edition ran
15 March – 15 June 2026, so **the next window is likely early 2027**.

Source: cnpdpcj.gov.pt/selo-protetor

### Great Websites for Kids (ALSC/ALA) — **retired**

The programme no longer exists. It was retired and folded into the ALSC
**Notable Children's Digital Media** list, whose committee evaluates all forms
of children's digital media rather than websites alone.

**The successor is the real target**, if a US audience matters. It is a
selection list rather than a certification — there is no seal to display and no
audit to pass, but inclusion carries genuine weight with librarians, teachers
and parents in the US.

Source: gws.ala.org/about · ala.org/alsc

## What is worth pursuing instead

### kidSAFE Seal Program — the closest thing to what was wanted

A certification service designed specifically for children's websites, apps and
online services, run by Samet Privacy, with an FTC-approved COPPA safe-harbour
tier. Three layers:

| Seal | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| kidSAFE LISTED | the product is designed for children, families or schools |
| kidSAFE+ COPPA CERTIFIED | verified COPPA compliance, with safe-harbour protection |
| AI seal | age-appropriate, ethically designed AI experiences |

**Two things to weigh.** It is US-centred: COPPA is American law, and the
certification matters most if ColorHugs has US users. And it is a paid,
annually audited programme — a real cost, not a form to fill in.

**The AI seal is worth noting now**, because Imagine & Create is exactly the
kind of feature it exists to assess, and designing that feature with the
criteria in view is far cheaper than adapting it afterwards.

*Alternative in the same space:* iKeepSafe, also an FTC-approved COPPA safe
harbour.

Source: kidsafeseal.com · ftc.gov

### For the European market

There is no EU equivalent of kidSAFE with the same recognition. What exists:

- **GDPR compliance**, which is not optional and not a seal, though Article 42
  certification schemes exist and are worth asking a data-protection lawyer
  about.
- **Better Internet for Kids (BIK)**, the EU network SeguraNet belongs to —
  visibility and partnership rather than certification.
- **Common Sense Media** reviews, influential with English-speaking parents.
  Not a seal, and reviews are not requested — but a good one travels.

## Proposed sequence

1. **Now, while building.** Design to the criteria: verifiable consent, data
   minimisation, no behavioural advertising, documented retention and deletion.
   Costs nothing extra if done from the start.
2. **Before launch.** Privacy policy, terms, retention schedule and consent
   flow reviewed by a lawyer. Everything the seals ask to see.
3. **Selo Protetor**, early 2027 window, for the practice.
4. **kidSAFE**, once there are real users and a budget, and only if the US
   market is being pursued.
5. **ALSC Notable Children's Digital Media**, once the platform has enough
   substance to be worth submitting.

---

## Warning

**Never display a seal that has not been earned**, and never imply endorsement
by an organisation that has not given it. On a product for children, sold on
trust, a false badge is not a marketing shortcut — it is the end of the
proposition. This includes phrasing like "built to eSafety standards" where no
such assessment took place.
